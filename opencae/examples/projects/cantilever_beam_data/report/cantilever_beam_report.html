Assumptions and differences

  • Euler-Bernoulli: plane sections remain plane and normal to the axis; shear deformation neglected.
  • Timoshenko: adds shear deformation with Cowper's shear coefficient k = 10(1+nu)/(12+11nu).
  • Small displacements, linear-elastic isotropic material.
  • Beam theory assumes the load acts at the section centroid and ignores how the end is clamped; the 3-D model fixes all displacements on the root face (which also suppresses Poisson contraction, slightly stiffening the root) and applies the load as a uniform traction on the tip face.
  • Beam theory gives the bending stress M*c/I away from the ends. The 3-D model has stress singularities at the corners of the fully clamped root face, so peak FE stress there grows with mesh refinement and must not be compared directly; compare stresses a section depth away from the root.

Limitations

  • Linear static analysis: small strains and rotations, no contact, no plasticity, no buckling check.
  • Nodal stresses are extrapolated from integration points and averaged by CalculiX; they smooth discontinuities. Raw integration-point maxima are reported separately.
  • Results depend on mesh density; perform a convergence study for design decisions.
  • Material values marked as examples must be verified.
