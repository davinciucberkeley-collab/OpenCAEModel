  • Euler-Bernoulli: plane sections remain plane and normal to the axis; shear deformation neglected.
  • Timoshenko: adds shear deformation with Cowper's shear coefficient k = 10(1+nu)/(12+11nu).
  • Small displacements, linear-elastic isotropic material.
  • Beam theory assumes the load acts at the section centroid and ignores how the end is clamped; the 3-D model fixes all displacements on the root face (which also suppresses Poisson contraction, slightly stiffening the root) and applies the load as a uniform traction on the tip face.
  • Beam theory gives the bending stress M*c/I away from the ends. The 3-D model has stress singularities at the corners of the fully clamped root face, so peak FE stress there grows with mesh refinement and must not be compared directly; compare stresses a section depth away from the root.
  • Slenderness L/h = 10: shear deformation adds ~0.8 % (Timoshenko). The fully clamped root (Poisson contraction suppressed) stiffens the 3-D model slightly, so the converged solid result lies close to Euler-Bernoulli and just below Timoshenko.
  • Linear tetrahedra (C3D4) are known to be overly stiff in bending (shear locking) and converge slowly; they are included to show this behaviour, not as a recommendation.

  • Richardson extrapolation and the grid convergence index (GCI, Roache) use the three finest meshes of each element type with h = (V / n_elements)^(1/3).
  • The peak von Mises stress sits at the corners of the fully clamped root face, a stress singularity: it keeps rising with refinement (see table) and is not a convergent quantity. Mid-span bending stress, away from the root and load introduction, converges.
  • A Richardson estimate is only meaningful in the asymptotic range. C3D4 results are still dominated by shear locking at these densities, so its extrapolated value and GCI are indicative only; the C3D10 sequence is the reference for the converged solution.
